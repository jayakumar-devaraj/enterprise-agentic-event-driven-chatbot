## What fails
- **Street addresses: 0 of 8 street parts redacted** (`742 Evergreen Terrace`, `221B Baker Street`
  and others). Only city names nearby were caught. The scrubber has no address recognizer.
- **Obfuscated identifiers: all 4 structured misses.** `jane dot doe at example dot com` and
  `john[at]example[dot]org` (only "jane dot doe" was taken as a name, so the domain leaked), a
  card spelled out in words, and `192 . 168 . 1 . 20`. Digits with spaces, dots or no separators
  were caught.
- **Places in non-English sentences:** Valencia, Lyon and Kraków were missed in the Spanish, French
  and Polish-named sentences. A Vietnamese name was missed and its city taken as a person. The
  model is English-only.
- **Ambiguous names:** "Hope" was missed. "Reading" (the town) was missed. "Paris Hilton", "Jordan"
  and "Victoria" were taken as places, which still redacts them.
- **Precision:** PERSON false positives include "Je m'appelle", "Email" and "Hà Nội". The two
  false positives in the negatives are `2.4.1.9` (a version, taken as an IP) and `404-555-1000` (an
  error code, taken as a phone).

## Disposition
No fix is made against this set: tuning on it would make its next score meaningless. Candidate
fixes need a fresh held-out set written before they are built: a street-address recognizer, an
obfuscated-email pattern (`at`/`dot`, `[at]`/`[dot]`), and possibly a multilingual NER model. Until
then the recall claims are scoped to what this set shows. Structured identifiers in plain formats
are reliably caught. Addresses, obfuscated forms and non-English place names are not.
